Late Entry in Looped Language Models

In looped Transformers, deep cached context substitutes for token-local early passes. Our converged decoder verifies candidate blocks through only the final K < T passes on the critical path, completes exact 1 → T KV caches as piggybacked batch rows at zero extra serial calls, and projects finished wake states inside the same output GEMM to unify greedy-exact (τ = 0) and high-speed lossy (τ = ∞) decoding in one CUDA graph.

Critical Path · Sec. 3.2

1. Late-Pass Block Verification

Whereas LoopSpec, WaveFront, DAS, and LoopAhead accelerate drafting via shallow initial passes while still verifying every token through all T passes, our (1+B)2 block compresses critical-path verification itself to the final K < T passes.

Background Wake · Sec. 3.3

2. Causal ⌈T / K⌉ Cache Completion

Because looped layers share weights across passes, tentative groups advance K true passes per step inside ongoing forward calls. Chronological ordering guarantees every group reads exact earlier-group pass-k KV states.

Proposition 1 · Sec. 3.4

3. Exactness & Strict Progress

Projecting finished T-pass wake rows inside the existing lm_head GEMM guarantees bit-for-bit T-pass greedy equivalence in exact arithmetic at τ = 0 (verified on 258/258 adversarial fp64 tests) and strict prefix progress i*+1 > u.

Core Scientific Finding · Section 2

Why Final Passes Beat Initial Passes, and Where Errors Hide

Raw late entry fails on unadapted checkpoints only because pass 4 expects layer-normalized states (norms 37–51) rather than raw embeddings (norm 0.76). Once lightweight Stage-2 fine-tuning aligns that interface, coherent pass-aligned (K, V) context substitutes for skipped local passes on >98% of positions, isolating residual discrepancies to the 6% uncertain tail.

Depth Scaling & Hardware Law · Sections 4.6 – 4.7

Deeper Recurrence Makes Wake Faster, and Roofline Explains Why

Evaluating the exact same Huginn-3.5B checkpoint across T ∈ {8, 16, 32, 64} (36 → 260 effective layers) shows that while full-depth K = T block speedup saturates flatly near 3.3×, Wake rollback rates drop 21.5× with depth, driving τ = 0 speedups to 8.42× (+153% over K = 64 block) and τ = ∞ to 10.57×.

Huginn-3.5B Across T ∈ {8, 16, 32, 64} (GSM8K, n = 1,319)

Speedup climbs with T as K = T/4 rollbacks fall 12.46 → 0.58 per 100 tokens

• Exact Wake K=T/4 (τ=0) ┄ Exact Wake K=T/2 ■ Lossy K=T/4 (τ=∞) ─ Full K=T block

At shallow T = 8, K = 2 is below the readout horizon (.030 lossy), yet τ = 0 Wake catches every slip to restore .262 (+23.2 pts) while throttling speed gracefully; for T ≥ 16, τ = 0 climbs past the K = T wall up to 8.42× (9.17× at τ = 0.25).

Physical H100 Roofline & Multi-Request Serving (C ∈ [1, 32])

tpass(M, C) = ((t0 + αkvC)p + (βtcM)p)1/p calibrated strictly on K = 4

• K=2 adaptive B∈{8,4,2} ─ K=3 adaptive B∈{8,4,2} ┄ Fixed K=4, B=8

Skipping T − K passes on rejected draft branches cuts per-step row-passes by 39% (198 vs. 324 at B = 8), sustaining 2.31× decode (1.67× end-to-end with online prefills) at C = 32 via pre-captured B = 8 → 4 → 2 graphs.
